Pranayama

Initially, Sheshadri and Harish begin Pranayama with an introduction and philosophy to provide students with an understanding of its methods and techniques, so that they can appreciate the depth of the practice.

Prana means universal vital force. Ayama means extension or expansion. The word Pranayama means “extension or expansion of the dimension of Prana”. It is a deliberate yogic technique involving the slowing down or regulation of the movement of inhalation and exhalation, including appropriate breath retentions.

Traditionally, it was believed that Pranayama should only be practiced by individuals who had years of experience with Asanas. This was based on the idea that a strong foundation in physical postures was necessary before engaging in Pranayama.

However, modern research has shown that Pranayama can be practiced by individuals at all levels, even beginners. Its benefits are profound, as it helps in balancing pranic energy, promoting mental well-being, reducing stress, and supporting spiritual growth. In fact, Pranayama is now recognized as a powerful practice for enhancing overall well-being, irrespective of one's experience with Asanas.
UNDERSTANDING THE PRACTICE

Components of Pranayama

01

Puraka | Inhalation

Puraka, or inhalation, is the conscious intake of breath.

02

Rechaka | Exhalation

Rechaka, or exhalation, is the conscious release of breath.

03

Antar Kumbhaka | Internal Retention

Antar Kumbhaka refers to retention of the breath after inhalation.

04

Bahir Kumbhaka | External Retention

Bahir Kumbhaka refers to retention of the breath after exhalation.
